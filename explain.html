How the model works

Bayesian updating, visually

A test result changes probability by multiplying the clinician’s pre-test odds by the test’s likelihood ratio. The Fagan nomogram is a graphic shortcut for that same calculation.

Back to calculator Example: 20% pre-test CXR LR+ 9.5
Example 20% → 70%

A positive chest radiograph with LR+ 9.5 moves a 20% pre-test probability to about 70% post-test probability.

Why likelihood ratios are used

Sensitivity and specificity describe test performance, but clinicians usually need to know how a result changes the probability for this patient. Likelihood ratios are useful because they update pre-test odds directly.

pre-test odds = p / (1 - p)
post-test odds = pre-test odds × LR
post-test probability = odds / (1 + odds)

Worked example

If the bedside pre-test probability is 20%, the pre-test odds are 0.20 / 0.80 = 0.25. A positive CXR modeled with LR+ 9.5 gives post-test odds of 0.25 × 9.5 = 2.375. Converting odds back to probability gives 2.375 / 3.375 = 70.4%.

Clinical interpretation

The nomogram and calculator demonstrate probability movement, not a validated patient-level treatment rule. Biomarkers and respiratory PCR results are especially context-dependent, and correlated tests should not be treated as fully independent.
